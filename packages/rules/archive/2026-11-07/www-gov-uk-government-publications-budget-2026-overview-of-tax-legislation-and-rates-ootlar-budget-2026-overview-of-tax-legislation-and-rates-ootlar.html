Introduction

This document sets out the detail of each tax policy measure announced at Budget 2026 and of previously announced measures that will be included in Finance Bill 2026-27. It is intended for tax practitioners and others with an interest in tax policy changes, especially those who will be involved in consultations both on the policy and on draft legislation. 

All measures listed below are applicable UK wide unless specified otherwise.  

The information in the document is set out as follows:

Chapter 1 contains details of all the measures that are included in Finance Bill 2026-27.

Chapter 2 contains details of measures which are part of Budget 2026 but are not in Finance Bill 2026-27.

Table 1 lists measures in this document without a corresponding announcement in Budget 2026.

Annex A provides tables of tax rates and allowances for the tax year 2026 to 2027 and the tax year 2027 to 2028.

Annex B provides a guide to the impact assessments set out in tax information and impact notes.

Chapter 1 — Finance Bill 2026-27

Personal Tax

1.1 Income Tax: Savings Income Tax Rates for tax year 2027-2028

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to increase the rates of Income Tax applicable to savings income. From 2027 to 2028, the savings basic rate will be 24%, the savings higher rate will be 44%, and the savings additional rate will be 49%. 

The changes will take effect from 6 April 2027. These rates apply across the UK.

1.2 Income Tax: Property rates for tax year 2027-28

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to increase the rates of Income Tax applicable to property income. From 2027 to 2028, the property basic rate will be 24%, the property higher rate will be 44%, and the property additional rate will be 49%. 

These rates will apply across England, Wales and Northern Ireland. The government has provided the devolved governments of Scotland and Wales with the ability to set property income rates in line with their current Income Tax powers. 

The changes will take effect from 6 April 2027.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Income Tax: changes resulting from new savings and property rates.

1.3 Income Tax charge for tax year 2027-28 and Rates of Income Tax for tax year 2027-28

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will legislate in Finance Bill 2026-27 to set the charge for Income Tax and the corresponding rates for tax year 2027 to 2028.  

Finance Bill 2026-27 will set:

  • the main rates for 2027 to 2028, which will apply to non-savings, non-property and non-dividend income of taxpayers in England, Wales, and Northern Ireland
  • the default rates for 2027 to 2028, which will apply to non-savings, non-property and non-dividend income of taxpayers who are not subject to the main rates of Income Tax, Welsh rates of Income Tax or the Scottish rates of Income Tax

Income Tax rates and rate limits for Scottish taxpayers are set by the Scottish Parliament. The UK rates are reduced by 10p in £1 for Welsh taxpayers, and the Welsh rates of Income Tax are set by the Senedd and added to the UK rates.

1.4 Pension Tax Allowance

As announced at Budget 2025, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to implement the Pension Tax Allowance.

The Pension Tax Allowance ensures pensioners whose total income is up to the value of the full new State Pension do not have the administrative burden of paying small amounts of Income Tax via Simple Assessment. It is available in tax years 2027 to 2028, 2028 to 2029 and 2029 to 2030.

The value of the Pension Tax Allowance is calculated by applying the basic rate of Income Tax (20%) to the difference between the full new State Pension and the Personal Allowance. For individuals with total income above the value of the full new State Pension, the Pension Tax Allowance will be withdrawn at a rate of £1 for every £3 of income above this level.

The changes will take effect from 6 April 2027.

The tax information and impact note for this measure will provide more information: Income Tax reduction for eligible pensioners.

1.5 Trading Allowance Increase

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to increase the Trading Allowance from £1,000 to £3,000. The allowance provides relief for trading and miscellaneous income and simplifies the system for those individuals who choose to use the relief, where the allowances cover all their relevant income before expenses, as they will no longer have to declare or pay tax on this income. The trading income reporting threshold for Self Assessment will also be increased to £3,000. These changes will take effect from 6 April 2027.

1.6 Changes to Employee Share Schemes

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to make changes to the Company Share Option Plan (CSOP) and Enterprise Management Incentives (EMI) schemes.

  • The CSOP individual option limit will be increased to £120,000.
  • The interaction between the CSOP and EMI individual options limits will be removed.
  • The CSOP exercise period will be extended to 15 years.
  • The period for certain tax-advantaged and non-tax advantaged shares and share options reserved by third parties to be excepted from the disguised remuneration rules will also be extended to 15 years.
  • The exception for Employee Ownership Trusts (EOTs) in the EMI rules will be extended to companies arranging to become controlled by an EOT.
  • The EMI exercise window following disqualifying events will be extended to 180 days.

The changes will take effect from 6 April 2027.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Changes to employee share schemes, including Company Share Option Plan and Enterprise Management Incentives.

1.7 Enterprise Management Incentives: Changes to the process to notify a grant of options

As announced at Budget 2025, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to remove the requirement for a company to submit a separate notification of a grant of EMI option to HMRC. Instead, a company will be required to notify such a grant through the end-of-year return.

The changes will take effect for options granted on or after 6 April 2027.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Enterprise Management Incentives (EMI): simplification of the process to grant options.

1.8 Legislation to Define the Tax Treatment of Image Rights Payments

As announced at Budget 2025, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to clarify that image rights payments related to an employment are treated as taxable employment income. 

These payments will be subject to Income Tax and National Insurance Contributions (NICs). 

The changes will take effect from 6 April 2028 rather than 6 April 2027 as previously announced.   

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Income Tax treatment of image rights payments connected to employment.

1.9 Social Security Benefits: Clarification of Income Tax treatment

The government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to clarify the basis of taxation for some  social security benefits. This will ensure that the relevant social security benefits are taxed on the accruals basis. The legislation will also remove or update references to obsolete and renamed benefits in Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003.

The legislation will take effect from 6 April 2027.

1.10 Income Tax: Certificate of sponsorship fee and immigration skills charge

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to exempt from Income Tax an employer’s payment of a fee for a certificate of sponsorship or an immigration skills charge, which employers are required to pay when sponsoring a worker under the Skilled Worker route. The changes will apply to payments made on or after 28 October 2026.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Income Tax treatment of immigration skills charge and certificate of sponsorship fees.

1.11 Deduction of Income Tax at Source: informant rewards paid by HMRC

As announced at Budget 2025, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 requiring HMRC to deduct Income Tax at the additional rate from rewards paid to informants under the Strengthened Reward Scheme. Any further tax or repayment due will be reconciled in the usual way. The change will take effect from the date of Royal Assent to Finance Bill 2026-27.

The previously published tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Withholding of tax for rewards received under the Strengthened Reward Scheme - GOV.UK

1.12 Consequential Amendments arising from Changes to Savings and Property Rates of Income Tax

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to make amendments following the changes to the savings rates of Income Tax and the introduction of the property rates of Income Tax that were announced in Budget 2025. The amendments will ensure that the rules on insurers’ reporting, tax treated as paid on gains from certain foreign life insurance policies, deduction at source, calculations of trustees’ tax pools and the Transfer of Assets Abroad legislation operate as intended from 6 April 2027.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Income Tax: changes resulting from new savings and property rates.

1.13 Department for Energy Security and Net Zero (DESNZ) Electricity Bill Discount Scheme

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will legislate in Finance Bill 2026-27 to exempt payments made under the DESNZ Electricity Bill Discount Scheme from Income Tax, with retrospective effect from 6 April 2026. 

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Tax treatment of payments under the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero Electricity Bill Discount Scheme.

1.14 Cancelled Shifts: Technical Update

As announced at Budget 2025, payments provided for cancelled, moved, or curtailed shifts are taxable as employment income.  

The government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to ensure that payments made to agency workers for cancelled, moved or curtailed shifts are also covered by existing tax legislation.

1.15 Statutory Neonatal Care Pay (repeal of duplicate legislation)

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to remove legislation introduced by HMRC, which duplicated earlier legislation introduced by the then Department for Business and Trade, confirming that Statutory Neonatal Care Pay is a taxable benefit. This measure will remove HMRC’s legislation and treat it as never having had effect.

1.16 HMRC Definition of Terminal Illness for Pensions Access

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to allow a serious ill-health lump sum to be paid where the pension scheme member can be reasonably expected to live for less than a year. This will apply to uncrystallised rights regardless of whether the member has reached the age of 75 (so whether it is taxable or not).

The measure will also amend the definition of a registered medical practitioner. This will allow a medical practitioner based outside the UK to provide medical evidence of a terminal diagnosis if the scheme member is living in the country where the practitioner practises medicine.

The change will take effect from the date of Royal Assent to Finance Bill 2026-27.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Income Tax: changes to the definition of serious ill health lump sums and medical evidence requirements.

1.17 Collecting Tax Due on Excess Lump Sum Death Benefits

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to provide HMRC with a practical, standalone process for assessing and collecting tax due on lump sum death benefits. Where a member who died before age 75’s available lump sum and death benefit allowance is exceeded, the excess will no longer be treated as pension income of individual beneficiaries. Instead, a tax charge will be raised against those beneficiaries on their portion of the excess by reference to their marginal rate of Income Tax for the year the lump sum was paid.  

The measure will make consequential amendments to preserve the same Income Tax relief following this change where the benefit is subject to both inheritance tax and Income Tax, and will ensure that any refund of inheritance tax is brought into charge appropriately. 

The measure will also make further, minor technical amendments governing the calculation of transitional disregards following the abolition of the lifetime allowance.  

There are a number of differing effective dates for this measure. The measure will have retrospective effect for relevant lump sum death benefits paid on or after 6 April 2024. Transitional provisions will apply the same approach to relevant payments made in the 2023 to 2024 tax year. Consequential changes concerning the interaction with Inheritance Tax will apply in relation to deaths occurring on or after 6 April 2027. Amendments to the lifetime allowance disregards will also have effect from 6 April 2024.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Income Tax: assessing and collecting tax on lump sum death benefits above the allowance.

1.18 Tax Treatment of Pension and Lump Sum Death Benefits Under the Two-Year Rule

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to remove the two-year rule for pension and lump sum death benefits.

This will mean that tax-free pension and lump sum death benefits will no longer become taxable solely because they are paid more than two years after the pension scheme administrator first knew, or could reasonably have been expected to know, of the member’s death. The change will apply both where a payment would otherwise be taxable as pension income of the recipient and where it would otherwise be subject to the special lump sum death benefits charge. The measure will have effect in relation to payments made on or after 28 October 2026.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Income Tax: removing the pensions tax two-year rule.

1.19 Defined Benefit Pensions: Surplus Extraction Tax Regime

As announced at Autumn Budget 2025, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to enable defined benefit occupational pension schemes to make direct payments of surplus assets to members, where permitted by scheme rules and subject to trustee discretion. These authorised member surplus payments will be taxed as pension income where specified conditions are met, including requirements relating to the scheme’s funding position and the member having reached Normal Minimum Pension Age or, where applicable, their protected pension age. The legislation will include safeguards to prevent avoidance. The change will take effect from 6 April 2027.

1.20 National Savings and Investment Tracing Issue

As announced on 19 May 2026, the government will introduce legislation to prevent estates affected by the National Savings & Investments (NS&I) tracing error in respect of bereavement claims from facing disproportionate administrative burdens. The announcement confirmed that a full inheritance tax exemption would be introduced for holdings of the remediation population affected by the tracing error, and an Income Tax easement would be introduced for personal representatives in respect of interest accrued before death or during the administration period. The Finance Bill 2026-27 measure will provide a regulation-making power to enable the detail of the intended reliefs to be delivered through secondary legislation. Regulations may have retrospective effect, provided that they do not increase a person’s liability to tax.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: National Savings and Investments tracing issue: tax treatment.

1.21 Withholding Tax Exemption for Scottish Government Bonds

The government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to provide an exemption from the duty to deduct sums representing Income Tax from interest paid on Scottish Government bonds, the issuance of which is subject to Treasury approval. The exemption will apply to any interest paid on or after 28 October 2026.

1.22 High Value Council Tax Surcharge

As announced at Budget 2025, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to establish the High Value Council Tax Surcharge which will be an additional charge on residential properties worth £2m or more. Changes will take effect from April 2028. 

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: High Value Council Tax Surcharge on residential properties.

1.23 Reforming the Cultural Gift Scheme

As announced at Tax Update 2025, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to allow jointly owned property to be donated under the Cultural Gift Scheme and the relief to be calculated by reference to a donor’s beneficial entitlement to that property. The measure will also allow donors one opportunity within the five-year limit period to amend their remaining tax credit allocation.  These changes will take effect from 6 April 2027.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Cultural Gift Scheme reform.

1.24 The Introduction of Mandatory Payrolling of Benefits in Kind from April 2027

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to require employers to report and payroll most benefits in kind and taxable expenses through Real Time Information (RTI). For company cars and vans, car and van fuel and medical benefits the changes will take effect from 6 April 2027. All other benefits in kind, with the exception of employment-related loans and employer-provided living accommodation, will take effect from 6 April 2028.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Changes to reporting of benefits in kind from April 2027.

1.25 Taxation of Company Cars: The appropriate percentage for tax years 2030 to 2031

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to set Company Car Tax rates for the tax year 2030 to 2031.

Appropriate percentages for zero emission vehicles will increase by 1 percentage point in the tax year 2030 to 2031.

Appropriate percentages for all other vehicle bands will increase by 1 percentage point per year in the tax year 2030 to 2031, to a maximum appropriate percentage of 40%. 

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Income Tax: company car tax rates for 2030 to 2032.

1.26 Inheritance  Tax: Preventing Unintended Deductions for Liabilities in Trust Arrangements

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to amend the inheritance tax rules on liabilities and trust property.   The legislation will provide a statutory rule for taking liabilities properly incurred by trustees into account when valuing settled property for inheritance tax purposes, align their treatment with personal liabilities and extend existing debt deduction restrictions and anti-avoidance rules to ensure that certain liabilities funded using value originating from a beneficiary’s own estate cannot reduce inheritance tax charges. The measure will have effect for deaths and other chargeable events occurring on or after 28 October 2026.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Inheritance Tax: preventing unintended deductions for liabilities in trust arrangements.

1.27 Inheritance Tax: Minor technical amendment(s) to extension of Inheritance Tax to pensions from April 2027

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to allow a prospective personal representative to direct pension scheme administrators to pay the inheritance tax due to HMRC when certain conditions are met.

Most unused pension funds and pension death benefits will be brought within the value of a person’s estate for inheritance tax purposes from 6 April 2027. This was legislated for in Finance Act 2026.

The government will also introduce legislation allowing those administering an estate to decide whether notional pension property included in the ‘general’ component of the estate or as a separate component for the purposes of calculating the reduced rate of inheritance tax when part of the estate is left to charity.

The changes will take effect from 6 April 2027. There is no tax information and impact note for this measure, the changes made here do not affect the impacting published in the 2025 tax information and impact note published here: Inheritance Tax — unused pension funds and death benefits - GOV.UK .

1.28 Exit Charges for Temporarily Migrating Trusts

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to provide an administrative easement to the Capital Gains Tax ‘exit charge’ that otherwise applies when a trust ceases to be resident in the United Kingdom. An exit charge will not apply when a trust becomes UK resident because one of its trustees moved to the UK; and it ceases to be UK resident later that year or the following year. This will take effect from 28 October 2026.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Capital Gains Tax exit charges for temporarily migrated trusts.

1.29 Capital Gains: Gains of offshore companies attributed to UK residents etc : protected cell companies

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to amend the capital gains participator rules for non-resident close companies, to ensure that they apply to protected cell companies. These changes will take effect from 28 October 2026.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Capital gains: preventing protected cell company avoidance.

1.30 Capital Sums in Return for Surrender of Rights

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to amend the capital gains legislation to provide that a capital sum received under a contract for loss of right under that contract is in scope. This will take effect from 28 October 2026.  

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Capital Gains Tax — capital sums received for surrendering rights.

1.31 Capital Gains Tax: Late filing penalties for returns for disposals of UK land

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to make changes to the late filing penalties regime that applies where a person fails to submit a UK property disposal return reporting a residential property disposal within the statutory deadline. This will stop those penalties continuing to accrue where that person later reports the disposal on their Self-Assessment return.

This change will take effect from 28 October 2026

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Capital Gains Tax: returns for disposals of UK land and late filing penalties.

1.32 Capital Gains Tax: Introduction of a new relief for founders

As announced at Budget 2026, the government is introducing a new Capital Gains Tax (CGT) Founders’ Relief. This measure launches a technical consultation on the relief and is being published alongside the Summary of Responses to the Call for Evidence on tax support for entrepreneurs.

The relief will support founders and early investors who have made a significant long-term contribution to the creation and development of the UK’s most high-growth and innovative companies. The relief will provide a lower rate of CGT, for qualifying disposals of shares or securities, on or after 6 April 2027.

The consultation seeks views on the practical application of the relief and will close on 30 November 2026. The government will then introduce legislation through amendments to Finance Bill 2026-27 so that Founders’ Relief can take effect for qualifying disposals made on or after 6 April 2027.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Capital Gains Tax: introduction of a founder relief.

1.33 Capital Gains Tax: Business Asset Disposal Relief lifetime limit reduction

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 reducing the lifetime limit of how much Business Asset Disposal Relief (BADR) an individual can claim from £1,000,000 to £500,000 for BADR qualifying disposals made on or after 6 April 2027.

There are special provisions that apply to contracts entered into before 6 April 2027 that have not been completed, and for certain share reorganisations and exchanges of securities not completed, before 6 April 2027.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Capital Gains Tax: reduction in the Business Asset Disposal Relief lifetime limit.

1.34 Capital Gains Tax Relief for Gifts of Business Assets

As announced at Budget 2025, the government will introduce legislation in the Finance Bill 2026-27 to modernise the formula that restricts gift holdover relief on the disposal of qualifying shares or securities, to include assets within the intangible fixed assets regime or that qualify for the substantial shareholding exemption. This will take effect from 6 April 2027.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Capital Gains Tax relief on gifts of business assets.

1.35 Tax Treatment of Stablecoins

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to treat eligible stablecoins more like money for tax purposes. These changes will amend tax rules for individuals, trustees and companies, and extend the application of withholding tax. These changes will take effect from April 2027.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Tax treatment of stablecoins.

1.36 Changes to Venture Capital Schemes

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to fix minor technical issues affecting the operation of the Seed Enterprise Investment Scheme (SEIS), Enterprise Investment Scheme (EIS) and Venture Capital Trust (VCT) rules.

The amendment to the VCT relief withdrawal rules will take effect for relevant disposals of shares issued on or after 6 April 2026, and the rest of the changes will take effect from 6 April 2027.

The government will also engage stakeholders on improving Advance Assurance and enhancing transparency of fees, alongside clearer HMRC guidance and greater compliance activity on fee arrangements that divert investment away from qualifying companies.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Changes to the Seed Enterprise Investment Scheme, Enterprise Investment Scheme and Venture Capital Trusts.

Corporate Tax 

1.37 Increase in Rate of Banking Surcharge for Temporary Period 

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to temporarily increase the rate of the Corporation Tax surcharge on banking companies to 6%, for 15 months.  

The changes will apply from 1 January 2027 to 31 March 2028.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Increase in the banking surcharge rate for a temporary period.

1.38 Financial Year 2028 Charge and Rate of Corporation Tax

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to set the charge for Corporation Tax as it does every year, and to maintain the main rate at 25% and the small profits rate at 19%, for the financial year beginning 1 April 2028.

1.39 Corporation Tax: Foreign permanent establishment (PE) exemption

As announced on 21 May 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to make the foreign permanent establishment (PE) exemption regime mandatory. The changes will take effect for Corporation Tax accounting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Reform of the foreign permanent establishment exemption.

1.40 Pillar 2: Side-by-Side Package and Further Amendments

As announced on 13 July 2026 and confirmed at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to amend Multinational Top-up Tax and Domestic Top-up Tax. The amendments implement the OECD’s Side-by-Side package and make further technical amendments to ensure the rules work effectively.

The Side-by-Side package will take effect for accounting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2026. Most of the technical amendments in this measure will take effect for accounting periods beginning on or after 31 December 2026, although will be permitted to take effect from an earlier date by taxpayer election. Certain other technical amendments will apply for accounting periods beginning on or after 31 December 2023 or 31 December 2024.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Introduction of the Side-by-Side package and amendments to Multinational Top-up Tax and Domestic Top-up Tax.

1.41 Advance Tax Certainty Service: Threshold Reduction

Following the launch of the Advance Tax Certainty Service in July 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to lower the entry threshold for qualifying projects from £1 billion to £300 million. In addition, the government will:

  • Amend the procedure for further changes to the definition of ‘qualifying investment projects’ from the draft affirmative Statutory Instrument to the negative procedure.
  • Amend the penalty provision at section 270, Finance Act 2026 so that in respect of a failure to provide information a penalty will only be charged where it relates to a clearance that has been issued. The other penalty provisions have not been changed.

The changes relating to the threshold level and penalty rules will take effect on 4 November 2026. Changes relating to the power will take effect from 1 April 2027. The service will be kept under review with a view to developing it further in the future.

1.42 Capital Allowances: Reduce the main rate writing-down allowance and increase the main rate first-year allowance

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to increase the main rate first-year allowance to 50% for expenditure incurred on or after 1 January 2027 and to reduce the main rate writing-down allowance to 12%, to take effect from 1 April 2027 for Corporation Tax and 6 April 2027 for Income Tax.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Reduce the main rate writing-down allowance and increase the main rate first-year allowance.

1.43 Corporate Interest Restriction: Interaction with Creative Reliefs

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to make technical amendments to Corporate Interest Restriction (CIR). From 1 January 2027, the calculation of taxable earnings for CIR will exclude Audio-Visual Expenditure Credits and Video Games Expenditure Credits.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Corporate Interest Restriction — interaction with creative reliefs.

1.44 Investment Life Insurance Contracts

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to make it explicit that the existing exemption from Corporation Tax for companies receiving payouts under Investment Life Insurance Contracts is only available when the contract is held otherwise than for the purposes of a trade the company carries on.

This will take effect for accounting periods beginning on  or after 1 January 2027.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Corporation Tax treatment of investment life insurance contract payouts.

1.45 Oil and Gas Revenue Levy (OGRL)

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to establish the Oil and Gas Revenue Levy (OGRL), a new, permanent additional 35% levy on exceptional oil and gas revenues arising during periods of unusually high prices.

The OGRL will apply to exceptional oil or gas revenues arising on or after the day immediately following the cessation of the Energy Profits Levy (EPL), which will be 1 April 2030 or an earlier date if the Energy Security Investment Mechanism (ESIM) is triggered.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Oil and gas taxation — Oil and Gas Revenue Levy.

1.46 Exploration or Exploitation Rights

As announced in July 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to explicitly treat exploration and exploitation rights in relation to activities undertaken in the UK or on the UK Continental Shelf as immovable property. The changes will take effect for accounting periods beginning on or after 13 July 2026 for Corporation Tax and for profits arising from 6 April 2027 for Income Tax.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Exploitation rights.

1.47 Research and Development (R&D) Tax Reliefs: Improving the Customer Experience

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 to remove the requirement to submit a Claim Notification Form for R&D claims made on or after 1 April 2027, and will simplify and rationalise the information required in the Additional Information Form for companies that claim R&D tax relief from autumn 2027.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Research and Development (R&D) Tax Reliefs: improving the customer experience.

1.48 Cryptoasset Loans and Liquidity Pools

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 for cryptoasset loans and liquidity pools. Certain transfers by individuals and trustees into and out of qualifying arrangements will be on a ‘no gain no loss’ basis until an economic disposal. The change will take effect from 6 April 2027.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Cryptoasset loans and liquidity pools.

1.49 Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986: Disqualification Following Breach of HMRC Securities Legislation

2.7 Approved Mileage Allowance Payments (AMAPs) and Self-Employed Simplified Mileage Rates

As announced on 27 October, the government will increase the Approved Mileage Allowance Payments (AMAPs) and Simplified Mileage Rates for business mileage above 10,000 miles from 25 pence to 35 pence per mile.

The changes will take effect from 6 April 2027 and will also apply to the corresponding National Insurance Contributions disregard for Relevant Motoring Expenditure (RME).

The government will introduce legislation by statutory instrument later in the year to uprate these charges ahead of the tax year 2027 to 2028.

2.8 Reform of Benchmark Scale Rates and Overseas Scale Rates

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will reform and simplify Benchmark Scale Rates (BSR) and Overseas Scale Rates (OSR) which are used by employers to reimburse their employees for domestic and overseas travel and subsistence expenses, without the need to check receipts. 

BSR will be modernised to better reflect current subsistence costs. The new rates will be £20 for periods of travel over 5-hours, £60 for over 10-hours and £75 for over 24-hours.  

The OSR will be simplified replacing the 200+ destination-specific OSRs with a simplified tiered approach that links to the updated BSR. 

The government will introduce the necessary secondary legislation before its implementation from 6 April 2028. 

Ahead of publication, HMRC will continue to engage with interested stakeholders to finalise the appropriate OSR on a destination-by-destination basis and the associated guidance. 

2.9 Reforming how Taxpayers Claim for Employment Expenses

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will remove the £2,500 Income Tax Self Assessment expenses threshold from April 2028, allowing more taxpayers to claim tax relief on non-reimbursed employment expenses through PAYE.

This change will simplify the process for taxpayers by removing the need to enter Self Assessment solely to claim employment expenses, and ensure more tax relief claims are subject to evidence before relief is issued.  This will make it easier for HMRC to administer claims and help to ensure tax relief goes to those who are entitled to it.

The government will keep under review whether there is a case for mandating the use of the PAYE route for taxpayers solely claiming employment expenses.

2.10 Guided Retirement and Retirement Collective Money Purchase (CMP)

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in a future Finance Bill to support consolidation of Defined Contribution (DC) pensions and the introduction of Guided Retirement and Retirement-only Collective DC (RCDC) schemes. Legislation will amend the conditions in which an individual may keep their pension age or lump sum protection following a transfer of their pension rights between pension schemes. The legislation will also enable individuals who start receiving a pension under a RCDC scheme to convert that pension to a drawdown pension within a set period, if the RCDC scheme chooses to provide that option. Other legislative changes will be made to ensure the benefit payment rules can operate as intended for RCDC schemes and to allow transfers from drawdown funds to RCDC schemes.

2.11 Treatment of Welsh Foundation Apprenticeships in Child Benefit

The government no longer treats Welsh Foundation Apprenticeships as approved training for the purpose of determining Child Benefit entitlement in respect of a young person aged 16-19. This aligns treatment of such apprenticeships with existing legislation. This change took effect from 9 September 2026. 

Corporate Tax 

2.12 Corporate Tax Simplification for Business Investment

As announced at Budget 2026, the government is publishing a consultation, Corporate Tax Simplification for Business Investment, which identifies areas of the Corporate Tax system where targeted simplification could improve certainty and support investment, growth and competitiveness. The consultation sets out potential areas for reform and identifies opportunities to simplify aspects of the Corporate Tax regime while maintaining its effectiveness and integrity. The government will work with stakeholders over the next year to develop and prioritise reforms.

2.13 Corporation Tax revenue recognition deferral for gas network operators

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will legislate in Finance Bill 2027-28 to spread certain Corporation Tax impacts of changes to revenue recognition requirements for qualifying gas distribution activities evenly over five years. Spreading the tax impact over this period will help smooth out any resulting financial impacts.

The measure will apply to qualifying revenues which would be passed on to consumers, where these were previously deferred and now recognised as a result of changes to accounting standards and brought into account for Corporation Tax. The transitional amount, which would otherwise be taxable in 2026-27, will be spread over five years from 2026-27. The precise tax outcomes and qualifying transitional amount will be set out in due course. This will not reduce the overall Corporation Tax due.

2.14 Reform of Advance Corporation Tax (ACT) regime

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in a future Finance Bill to repeal the remaining Advance Corporation Tax (ACT) regime with effect from 1 April 2031. All remaining unrelieved surplus ACT balances will be extinguished when the regime ends. Further details are set out in the government’s response to the Advance Corporation Tax Reform consultation, published at Budget 2026.

2.15 Modernising the Distributions Framework

As announced at Budget 2026, the government has published a summary of responses to the consultation on modernising the distributions framework: Modernising the taxation of distributions and repayments of capital from companies - GOV.UK which was published at Tax Update 2026.

The government is proceeding with the proposals, with some changes to the policy design informed by stakeholder feedback. Further engagement will focus on the technical detail, and draft legislation will be published in 2027 for technical consultation.

The changes will take effect from April 2028.

2.16 Modernising taxation of foreign sovereign investments

As announced at Budget 2026, the government has published a consultation and will introduce legislation in a future Finance Bill for foreign sovereigns investing in the UK.

This will legislate a new sovereign tax status available for states, heads of state and emanations of states, while bringing certain income and gains into direct tax (Corporation Tax, Income Tax and Capital Gains Tax). Investment management activities undertaken by sovereign investment offices and gains arising to State-operated Overseas Pension Funds will be exempted from tax.

Sovereigns will keep qualifying institutional investor status for the Real Estate Investment Trust (REIT), Qualifying Asset Holding Company, Long-Term Asset Fund and most collective investment vehicle regimes. Sovereigns will get bespoke access to a REIT structure which will enable them to hold a controlling interest in a REIT through a UK company while benefiting from a 15% tax rate on property income distributions.  Transitional provisions will include rebasing for certain assets and a time-limited Stamp Duty Land Tax relief for restructuring of certain existing property holdings, which will apply ahead of the reforms taking effect.

The government will publish draft legislation for technical consultation.

The reforms will take effect from 1 January 2029.

2.17 Annual Tax on Enveloped Dwellings (ATED): Annual chargeable amounts

As announced at Budget 2026, the ATED annual charges will rise by 3.5% from 1 April 2027 in line with the September 2026 Consumer Prices Index (CPI).

ATED 2027 to 2028 charges are set out in Annex A.

Indirect Tax

2.18 Fuel Duty Rates

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation to extend the temporary 5 pence per litre (ppl) cut in the main rates of fuel duty (originally introduced at Spring Statement 2022) from 1 January 2027 until 30 June 2027.  In addition, the legislation will extend the further 3.7 ppl cut to the rebated rates for gas oil (red diesel), biodiesel, bioblend and kerosene for excepted machines other than for heating, until 30 June 2027.  This legislation will take effect from 1 January 2027.  The government will also introduce legislation to increase fuel duty rates in line with Retail Prices Index (RPI) inflation from 1 July 2027. 

On 1 July 2027, fuel duty rates will return to the levels that applied before the 5 ppl temporary cut was introduced in March 2022, and will be uprated in line with the Retail Prices Index (RPI).

2.19 Machine Games Duty: Changing who is liable for the duty

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce legislation in a future Finance Bill to make machine suppliers liable for Machine Games Duty on supplier-owned machines operated under profit-share arrangements. Premises licence holders, permit holders and pub tenants will remain liable where they own the machine outright or use it under a fixed rental or lease arrangement and control the takings. The government is consulting on the implementation of this change until 20 January 2027. 

The change will take effect from 1 April 2028.

2.20 Extending the Temporary 0% VAT rate for Domestic Electricity

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will extend the temporary VAT zero rate for domestic electricity supplies until 30 June 2027. A 0% rate of VAT will continue to apply to qualifying electricity supplies that would otherwise be subject to the reduced rate of 5% from 1 April 2027. The government will lay secondary legislation before Parliament in early 2027 to extend this temporary zero rate of VAT.

2.21 Temporary reduced rate of VAT on family activities (Great British Summer Savings)

As announced on 21 May 2026, a temporary reduced rate of VAT of 5% applied to children’s meals, children’s and family admission to cinemas and theatres, and admissions to qualifying family attractions between 25 June and 1 September 2026. The measure reduced VAT from the standard rate of 20% to 5% on eligible supplies during the school summer holiday period as part of the Great British Summer Savings package.

2.22 Energy-Saving Materials (ESM) VAT relief

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will lay secondary legislation before Parliament to include heat batteries within the VAT relief for energy-saving materials when installed in residential accommodation or charity buildings in the UK. This change will take effect from 1 April 2027. As announced when the temporary zero rate was introduced in 2022, the rate of VAT relief for energy-saving materials will revert to 5 per cent from 1 April 2027.

2.23 Vehicle Excise Duty Support for Heavy Goods Vehicles (Reduced Rate)

As announced on 20 May 2026, the government has provided a 12-month reduced £1 annual rate of Vehicle Excise Duty (VED) for certain HGVs. This was legislated in the summer through the Taxation (Energy and Vehicles) Act 2026 and applies from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027.

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Temporary Vehicle Excise Duty rates for heavy goods vehicles.

2.24 VAT: Zero Rate for Land Sold to a Relevant Housing Association

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will lay secondary legislation before Parliament in early 2027 to bring into effect a new zero rate of VAT on the sale of land to registered social housing providers (relevant Housing Associations). This change will take effect from 1 April 2027. 

A consultation was announced at Budget 2025 and the summary of responses to the consultation is being published at Budget 2026. [LINK to be added when published.] 

2.25 Review of the VAT treatment of travel services including the Tour Operators’ Margin Scheme

As announced at Budget 2026, the government has published a call for evidence for the review on the VAT treatment of travel services, including the Tour Operators’ Margin Scheme (TOMS).

It seeks views on whether TOMS remains fit for purpose and should be retained, reformed, simplified, made optional, partly withdrawn or removed.

The government is also reviewing the treatment of travel services outside of the TOMS that are enjoyed in the UK but supplied by tour operators based outside the UK.

The evidence will inform proposals to simplify VAT administration, support fair taxation and reduce distortions in the VAT system.

The call for evidence runs from 28 October 2026 to 31 January 2027.

2.26 Plastic Packaging Tax: Mechanically recycled plastic certification consultation and summary of responses

As announced at Budget 2026, the government has published a summary of responses to the May 2026 consultation on potential certification for mechanically recycled plastic packaging for the purposes of Plastic Packaging Tax (PPT). This confirms that the government will introduce mandatory certification for businesses claiming an exemption from PPT on the basis it meets the 30% recycled plastic threshold. This change will take effect from 1 April 2030.

2.27 Landfill Communities Fund Value for 2027 to 2028

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will increase the cap on the amount of tax credit claimable by landfill operators in respect of contributions made to the Landfill Communities Fund against their Landfill Tax liability. The cap will increase from 5.3% to 8.3% of their Landfill Tax liability, providing an estimated fund value of £25.3m for 2027 to 2028. The government will legislate for this measure through secondary legislation. This change will come into force from 1 April 2027.

2.28 Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM): Sectoral scope expansion to refinery products

As announced at Budget 2026, the government intends to expand the scope of the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) to include refinery products, subject to consultation on feasibility and impacts. The consultation opened on 28 October 2026 and will close on 20 January 2027.  The government will consider responses before bringing forward any legislation required to implement the change.

2.29 Gaming Duty: Gross Gaming Yield Bands

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will freeze the gross gaming yield bandings used to determine the rate of Gaming Duty from 1 April 2027 to 31 March 2028.

2.30 Nicotine Approval Scheme

As announced in April 2025, the government will consult on the design of a Nicotine Approval Scheme (NAS) to introduce controls on the supply of high-strength nicotine from 1 April 2028. The consultation has been published alongside Budget 2026 and will run for 12 weeks until 20 January 2027, inviting respondents to share views on technical details of the scheme including threshold, exemptions and enforcement options. 

The tax information and impact note for this measure provides more information: Nicotine Approval Scheme.

Tax Administration and Other Measures

2.31 More Timely Payments for Self Assessment 

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will require Income Tax Self Assessment taxpayers who make Payments on Account to make more regular in year payments towards their final tax bill from April 2029. Payments will be automated, with eligible taxpayers able to choose to pay through PAYE. The government consulted on the approach to more timely payment in Income Tax Self Assessment and is publishing a Summary of Responses alongside Budget 2026. The government will continue to work with taxpayers and stakeholders on detailed design and implementation.

2.32 Requiring Direct Debit arrangements for VAT and PAYE return liabilities

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will require taxpayers to set up a Direct Debit instruction for VAT and PAYE return liabilities from April 2029, subject to defined exceptions. Businesses will continue to be able to use other payment methods to pay their liability before a specified cut-off date. The Direct Debit instruction will be used to collect any unpaid balances. Legislation will be introduced at a future Finance Bill. The government has published its response to the consultation alongside this announcement.

2.33 Clamping Down on Till Fraud

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce mandatory software standards for Electronic Point of Sale and Mobile Point of Sale systems to tackle Electronic Sales Suppression (ESS) or ‘till fraud’. The government has published its Summary of Responses to the consultation on ESS, which was launched on 23 June 2026. HMRC will undertake further technical engagement with industry and publish a technical consultation in summer 2027, setting out detailed implementation proposals. The government intends to legislate in a future Finance Bill. The implementation date will be determined following further policy development and consultation.

2.34 Addressing Persistent Non-Compliance by a Small Minority of Larger Businesses

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will review its approach to addressing persistent non-compliance and egregious behaviour by a small minority of larger businesses. This review will consider the effectiveness of the existing framework for managing larger business compliance, including the Special Measures regime and other tools available to HMRC, to ensure they remain fit for purpose. The government will engage with stakeholders next year as it considers any future changes.

2.35 Removing the Financial Incentives of Tax Avoidance

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will shortly publish a consultation on a package of measures to tackle the promotion and use of tax avoidance, including:

  • new powers to tackle serious and serial users of tax avoidance schemes.
  • changes to enable earlier recovery of promoters’ and certain individuals’ liabilities.
  • changes to further strengthen HMRC’s existing Promoters of Tax Avoidance Schemes regime.

2.36 MTD for Income Tax: Penalty Easement April 2027 

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will not apply late submission penalty points to quarterly updates for the 2027-28 tax year for Income Tax Self-Assessment taxpayers required to join MTD in April 2027. Customers will still need to send their quarterly updates before they are able to submit their tax return.  

2.37 MTD for Income Tax: Exemptions  

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will exempt the following groups from MTD for Income Tax until further notice: those eligible for qualifying care relief, non-UK resident foreign entertainers and sportspeople and expected non-resident customers. Secondary legislation will be introduced before April 2027. 

As the government set out previously, some customers for whom HMRC cannot provide a digital service will not be required to use MTD for Income Tax - this will include a small number of insolvent customers who are not currently able to sign up. 

2.38 Summary of Responses to the Call for Evidence on Business Systems Integration

As announced at Budget 2026, the government has published a summary of responses to the business systems integration call for evidence carried out in 2026. The government will work with industry to test practical ways to improve integrations between business systems and support voluntary adoption. This intends to reduce administration burdens for businesses and improve record-keeping accuracy.

2.39 Call for Evidence: Modernising the Administration of Corporation Tax

As announced at Budget 2026, the government is publishing a Call for Evidence on the future administration of Corporation Tax. It seeks views on how to better support companies to report and pay the right amount of tax first time, while exploring opportunities for more integrated information flows, proportionate administrative requirements and better use of data. It also explores how advances in automation and artificial intelligence could support a modernised Corporation Tax administration system and what lessons can be learned from companies already using these technologies. This follows a commitment made in HMRC’s Transformation Roadmap, published in July 2025.

2.40 Modernising and Standardising Corporation Tax (CT) submissions

As announced at Budget 2026, the government is publishing a summary of responses to the consultation on Modernising and Standardising Corporation Tax Submissions published on 10 March 2026, which closed on 2 June 2026.  The government will proceed with prescribing Corporation Tax computations on a longer and more flexible implementation timetable. The government will also introduce mandatory online filing of amended Company Tax Returns from 1 April 2028 by directions.  Enforcement arrangements for prescribed computations will not be implemented at this time.

2.41 Summary of Responses to Tax Treatment of Predevelopment Costs Consultation

As announced at Budget 2026, the government has published a summary of responses to the consultation on the tax treatment of predevelopment costs and will work with stakeholders to explore what improvements can be made to HMRC guidance to provide greater clarity on the application of the existing capital allowances rules.

2.42 Offshore Penalty Reform Consultation

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will publish a consultation later this year on reform to the offshore penalties framework. Any legislation will be introduced in a future Finance Bill following consultation responses.

2.43 Improving Stewardship Through Better Quality Software Intermediation

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will introduce a package of measures to improve HMRC’s oversight of software intermediaries and strengthen requirements for software providers seeking to integrate with HMRC systems. The measures for software providers include: a registration process, strengthened standards including for software products, and proportionate enforcement for addressing breaches of HMRC’s software standards and Terms of Use.  HMRC will engage with the software industry as the detailed arrangements are developed.

2.44 Company Payments to Participators: Modernising the reporting framework

The government is publishing a summary of responses to the consultation on company payments to participators launched in March 2026, which closed in June 2026.

The government will not implement the new reporting requirements at this time. HMRC will continue to work with stakeholders on the concerns raised in the consultation to ensure customers can access the best information to help them meet their tax obligations.

2.45 Direct Recovery of Lower Value Tax Debts

As announced at Budget 2026, the government is publishing a summary of responses to the Tackling lower value tax debts consultation. The government will introduce legislation in a future Finance Bill to enable HMRC to recover established lower value tax debts through regular deductions from bank accounts where customers repeatedly fail to engage with HMRC. The changes are expected to take effect from 2029-30.

2.46 Investors in US Limited Liability Companies and other reverse hybrids

As announced at Budget 2026, the government will create a new double taxation relief mechanism for UK resident individuals who receive income from United States Limited Liability Companies and similar entities (‘reverse hybrids’) where relief is currently unavailable. A Summary of Responses to the initial consultation has been published, with a further technical consultation and draft legislation to follow in the coming months. Claims for the new form of credit relief are intended to be possible from early 2028.
